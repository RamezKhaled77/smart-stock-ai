# SmartStock AI — Glossary

## 1. Purpose

This document defines important business, inventory, data, and machine learning terms used throughout SmartStock AI.

The purpose is to keep terminology consistent across:

- Project documentation.
- Source code.
- Database fields.
- User interface labels.
- Machine learning components.
- Development discussions.

Use these definitions when naming variables, functions, database fields, and UI elements.

## 2. Inventory Terms

### Inventory

The collection of products and goods a business currently holds.

### Stock

The available quantity of a particular product.

### Current Stock

The quantity of a product currently recorded as available in inventory.

### Stockout

A situation where a product is unavailable when customers want to purchase it.

### Overstock

A situation where the business holds more stock than it reasonably needs.

### Reorder Point (ROP)

The stock level at which a business should consider placing a replenishment order.

A simplified formula is:

`ROP = Average Daily Demand × Lead Time + Safety Stock`

### Safety Stock

Additional inventory kept to reduce the risk of stockouts caused by unexpected demand or supply delays.

### Lead Time

The time between placing a replenishment order and receiving the products.

### Incoming Stock

Products that have been ordered but have not yet been received.

### Replenishment

The process of adding stock to inventory.

### Restocking

The action of replenishing product quantities.

### Order Quantity

The recommended quantity to purchase or replenish.

### Inventory Adjustment

A manual or system-recorded change to a product's stock quantity that is not necessarily caused by a normal sale.

## 3. Sales Terms

### Sales Record

A record describing the quantity of a product sold on a specific date or during a specific transaction.

### Sales Quantity

The number of units sold.

### Historical Sales

Previously recorded sales information.

### Daily Sales

The total quantity sold for a product on a particular day.

### Demand

The quantity of a product customers want to purchase during a specific period.

### Observed Sales

The quantity of a product actually sold.

Sales and demand are not always identical.

For example, if a product runs out of stock, observed sales may be lower than actual customer demand.

### Sales Trend

The general direction of sales activity over time.

## 4. Data Terms

### Dataset

A structured collection of data used for analysis or model training.

### Raw Data

Original data before cleaning or transformation.

### Processed Data

Data that has been cleaned, transformed, or prepared for a specific purpose.

### Data Cleaning

The process of identifying and handling missing, invalid, duplicate, or inconsistent data.

### Data Validation

Checking whether data satisfies required rules and constraints.

### Data Dictionary

A document describing dataset columns, types, meanings, and allowed values.

### Data Leakage

A problem that occurs when information unavailable at prediction time is accidentally used during model training or evaluation.

### Missing Value

A field that does not contain an available value.

### Outlier

A value that differs significantly from the general pattern of the data.

An outlier is not automatically an error; it must be investigated.

## 5. Machine Learning Terms

### Machine Learning (ML)

A field of artificial intelligence in which systems learn patterns from data to make predictions or decisions.

### Demand Forecasting

The process of estimating future product demand using historical information and other relevant variables.

### Forecast

An estimate of future demand.

### Forecast Horizon

The future period covered by a forecast.

For example, a seven-day forecast estimates demand over the next seven days.

### Target Variable

The value a machine learning model is trained to predict.

In this project, the target is expected to represent future sales quantity or an agreed demand estimate.

### Feature

An input variable used by a machine learning model to generate predictions.

### Feature Engineering

The process of creating useful model inputs from raw data.

### Lag Feature

A feature containing a previous observation.

Examples:

- Previous day's sales.
- Sales seven days ago.

### Rolling Mean

The average of observations over a moving time window.

Examples:

- Seven-day rolling average.
- Thirty-day rolling average.

### Baseline Model

A simple forecasting approach used as a reference when evaluating more advanced models.

### Training

The process of fitting a machine learning model using historical data.

### Prediction

The output generated by a trained model for new input data.

### Model Evaluation

The process of measuring how well a model performs on data not used for fitting it.

### MAE

Mean Absolute Error.

The average absolute difference between actual values and predicted values.

### RMSE

Root Mean Squared Error.

A metric that gives greater weight to larger prediction errors.

### Random Forest

An ensemble machine learning algorithm that combines multiple decision trees.

It is one of the candidate models under investigation, not an automatically accepted final model.

### Linear Regression

A regression algorithm that models relationships between input features and a numeric target.

It may be considered as a candidate model or baseline.

## 9. Important Distinctions

### Sales vs. Demand

Sales represent what was actually purchased.

Demand represents what customers wanted to purchase.

When products are unavailable, observed sales may underestimate actual demand.

### Prediction vs. Recommendation

A prediction estimates future demand.

A recommendation uses the prediction together with inventory information and business rules to suggest an action.

### Current Stock vs. Available Stock

Current stock is the recorded physical quantity.

Available stock may require subtracting reserved quantities or accounting for other business rules.

The initial application must define whether these concepts are treated as identical.

### Reorder Point vs. Order Quantity

The reorder point determines when replenishment should be considered.

The order quantity determines how much should be replenished.

They are different calculations.
